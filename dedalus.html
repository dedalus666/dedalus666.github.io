Now Entering: Piranesi

Five songs. Five labyrinths. No escape.

In the 18th century, the Italian architect Giovanni Battista Piranesi etched his Carceri d'invenzione, the Imaginary Prisons: vaulted chambers, stairways that lead nowhere, machinery without purpose. They were beautiful and terrifying. Piranesi, the newest Dedalus release, borrows those impossible architectures as its blueprint. Each of its five songs follows a character trapped in a labyrinth of their own, built from myth, magic, love, betrayal and loss.

  • Sky Woman. An Iroquois creation legend. A woman pregnant with twins is cast from an island in the sky by a jealous husband. The animals rescue her, and together they build the world out of mud on a turtle's back. Her sons Sapling and Flint give the world its rainbows and its thorns.
  • Curse of Jaxx! A town that fears what it doesn't understand murders a witch, and Jaxx! (J-A-double X, exclamation) answers with a curse of her own. The song features lead vocals by Yvonne Belle.
  • Rosabelle Believe. Before he died, Harry Houdini gave his wife Bess a secret code, Rosabelle, believe. Bess lit a candle for him every Halloween and waited for the message for the rest of her life. The song is Houdini's plea from the far side of the veil: proof that love may survive death even when it cannot get a word back through. Steve La Cerra plays drums, with haunting background vocals by Rowan Bishop.
  • Betrayer Moon. A fever dream. Hallucinations on loss, grief and betrayal under a moon that doesn't belong on the calendar.
  • Curse of Pasiphae. A five-part mythic suite running from The Minstrel's Prophecy to Citadel of Sorrow. A king cheats a god, a queen is cursed with a madness not her own, and a child, Asterios the Minotaur, is sealed inside a labyrinth built by Daedalus. But the wrath of the witch-queen Pasiphae is devastating. Her vengeance is not to be trifled with, as the adulterous King Minos discovers when his mistresses are felled by poisonous scorpions, delivered from his seed. Drums and percussion are by the ultra-talented Runtt Wah, former drummer of the now-disbanded East Coast band By Blood Alone.

All words and music are by joe Wilford, who also adds guitars, vocals, bass and keys. Rowan Bishop mixed Rosabelle Believe and Curse of Pasiphae at Garage Mahal Recording in Westbrook, Maine. Piranesi is the darkest and most theatrical record in the Dedalus catalogue so far, and its characters meet the fate this whole project has circled for two decades: the maze, its chambers and the long walk through its cavernous halls.

Piranesi will be released October 30, 2026, on Bandcamp, Spotify, Apple Music and all streaming platforms.

The Recent Past

A Thread in the Labyrinth is a full-length album of guitar instrumentals, released one song at a time from December 21, 2021 to December 1, 2022: thirteen tracks over two years. “The record is a project I've been wanting to do for years.” Each song tells a different story in its own sonic landscape. Where the Path Began

An Iowa native, joe Wilford spent more than fifteen years finding and then redefining his sound in the competitive club scene of New York City's Lower East Side. A raconteur-de-force with a 12-string guitar, he played in a wide range of projects, including The Poetry Revolution, Logic Downwards and, most notably, the Trailside Rangers. They played well-known venues across the tri-state area, including CBGB's, Rodeo Bar and The Continental. The Rangers earned critical acclaim, released three independent records and drew generous college-radio airplay across the US and abroad, but they never took their indie status to the next level. After eight years they disbanded peacefully in 1999 to pursue other musical avenues.

Eventually Wilford found his way back to the heartland and the vibrant music scene of Eastern Iowa. There he keeps writing, recording and experimenting across a range of genres: acoustic rock, black metal dirges, gypsy flamenco guitar instrumentals, and ambient electronica threaded with dubstep beats, darkwave tones and cinematic soundscapes.

Invocation (2012) is a sonic collage of electronica, darkwave and dubstep, full of literary and film ghosts. McCarthy's The Road appears in Jackstraw Land, Moby Dick in Ishmael and Alan Moore's Watchmen in Luna Jungle (Ozymandias remix). Baum's Oz appears in The Great & Terrible and Logan's Run in LastDay. The epic Samsara weaves vastly different styles into a single story of longing and lust.

Wild Angels (2010) takes a more straightforward path, but no less tortured a one. It has ten smoldering soliloquies, fraught with abstract gloom and apocalyptic drones, and features some of Wilford's most restrained vocals. The songs are radiant in their ability to find the beautiful within the bitterly cruel.

Santiago – se unio~ (2007) is an experiment in gypsy flamenco with a twist of Americana. It pairs Latin-inspired beats with singed melodies drawn from red desert dust and the nylon strings of a classical guitar.

A Portrait of the Artist (2004) has nine songs, each a personal tribute to Tom Gallison, Wilford's long-time friend, who tragically died in February 2000. The songs echo sadness and loss, and they also celebrate the creative spirit that inspired everyone who knew him.

Beyond the Music

Dedalus is the artistic persona of joe Wilford: storyteller, songwriter, musician, magician and worker of wood. Much of that wider world lives in Labyrinthine Letters, a creative portal and archive of poems, lyrics, stories, handmade boxes and explorations of the dark arts. Art is a maze. Lose yourself. Next on the horizon is Lord Jim, a musical stage adaptation of the Joseph Conrad novel.

The journey is still without maps. Open some doors. Welcome.
